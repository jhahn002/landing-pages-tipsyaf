# Page formats and 29 modules

Choose the page that fits why someone arrived. Keep the promise, offer, and next action consistent with the source of the visit.

## Choose a format

| Format | Useful when | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focused product page | The visitor wants to judge one product and buy | Product clarity, relevant proof, offer, selection, and buying details |
| Listicle | The visitor needs several distinct reasons to reconsider a category | Supported reasons, each with a buying implication |
| Best sellers bundle | Selection or assembling a routine is the obstacle | Contents, who it suits, actual bundle value and terms |
| Founder letter | The founder's choices explain the product's relevance | Authentic first-person material, product proof, and offer |
| Search-intent page | The query names a specific need or comparison | A direct answer, relevant details, and a useful next step |
| Social proof page | Credibility is the main obstacle | Relevant, attributable experiences plus product explanation |
| Sale collection | The visitor already intends to shop a promotion | Current eligibility, clear prices, product choice, and sale terms |
| Gift guide | The visitor needs help choosing for someone else | Recipient needs, budgets, contents, gifting constraints, delivery |
| Early access | The visitor wants notice of a specific opportunity | Clear signup promise, consent, real form destination, confirmation |

For Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Boxing Day, or another sale, verify dates and timezone, exclusions, discount mechanics, market, and availability. A Small Business Saturday story requires authentic business facts. Do not invent a founder's history or use a seasonal event as evidence of an offer.

## Pick the modules the argument needs

The numbering is a menu, not a required order. Select modules by the customer question they answer and the evidence available.

| # | Module | Job and useful input |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Promotion bar | State a verified offer or delivery message compactly |
| 2 | Header and navigation | Establish the real brand and give relevant routes |
| 3 | Hero | Identify the product, connect to the incoming promise, and clarify the next action |
| 4 | Product gallery | Show the product accurately through detail and context imagery |
| 5 | Benefit strip | Make distinct benefits easy to scan without restating the hero |
| 6 | Problem and solution | Recognize the buying problem and explain the product's response |
| 7 | Reasons list | Develop separate buying arguments, each with evidence and a payoff |
| 8 | How it works | Explain a verified mechanism in ordinary language |
| 9 | How to use | Clarify actual setup, routine, dose, care, or operation |
| 10 | Ingredients, materials, and specifications | Supply details needed to judge fit or understand a claim |
| 11 | Demonstration and results | Show the product doing its job, with conditions and limitations |
| 12 | Testing, certifications, and experts | Support a relevant claim with correctly scoped evidence |
| 13 | Press and authority | Use actual coverage or endorsements that apply to this product or brand |
| 14 | Featured testimonial | Address a particular doubt with an attributable customer experience |
| 15 | Reviews and UGC | Show relevant experiences without invented people, ratings, or aggregates |
| 16 | Comparison table | Compare decision-relevant facts on the same basis, using current sources |
| 17 | Founder and brand story | Explain product choices that matter to the buying decision |
| 18 | Purchase module | Present product, price, selection, terms, and action together |
| 19 | Bundles and quantity selectors | Show what each option costs and includes |
| 20 | Sale collection and product grid | Make an eligible assortment easy to scan and shop |
| 21 | Gift guide, finder, and quiz | Reduce selection work and deliver a useful result |
| 22 | Inclusions and gifts | Show exactly what arrives and what qualifies for a genuine gift |
| 23 | Value and cost per use | Explain value through a defensible calculation with comparable units |
| 24 | Delivery, returns, and guarantees | Resolve timing and risk with the merchant's actual policies |
| 25 | FAQ | Answer remaining objections without duplicating the entire page |
| 26 | Early-access signup | Capture permission with a clear promise and functional confirmation |
| 27 | Repeated and sticky CTA | Keep the next step available without covering content or controls |
| 28 | Deadline and inventory | Communicate a real current constraint and an honest expired state |
| 29 | Footer, contact, and policies | Provide credible business information, help, and policy links |

## Put the argument in order

Write a short plan with one row per section:

| Customer question | Headline or payoff | Evidence or asset | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The doubt this section resolves | What the visitor will understand | Verified fact, demonstration, authorized review, or interaction | Read, compare, select, or buy |

A listicle may explain the product, develop supported reasons, show proof, and present the offer. A sale collection may put the offer and product grid first. A founder letter needs an authentic letter before shopping mechanics dominate.

For each section, ask what the visitor understands after reading it that they did not before. Combine sections that repeat the same point. Repeated CTAs can help; repeated claims with different adjectives add little. If fewer than five distinct reasons are supported, recommend a shorter list rather than padding it.
