# Research the product, brand, and buyer

Start with the supplied product URL. Research enough to support a clear page argument, then build. A large research report is not the default deliverable.

## Establish the product facts

Read the official product page, relevant FAQ, shipping and returns policies, and any evidence linked to a proposed claim. Inspect the selected market, currency, variant, and purchase option.

Record the product name, intended use, specifications, price, contents, offer eligibility, purchase destination, and material terms. Verify dates and renewal terms separately from product supply or usage estimates. If a fact changes by selection, preserve that relationship in the page.

Keep a small source table in the page brief:

| Finding | Type | Source and access date | Limit or qualifier | Planned use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exact fact or concise paraphrase | Official fact, customer experience, or interpretation | Direct page URL or supplied file | Variant, sample, study conditions, missing access, or uncertainty | Headline, section, FAQ, or omit |

Open the source before relying on a search snippet. If two official locations disagree on a price, review count, or specification, record the conflict and resolve it with the user or omit that detail. Do not average conflicting numbers. Recheck volatile facts before release.

For health, safety, or performance claims, inspect the underlying evidence. Match the product, amount, population, duration, and outcome to what was actually tested. Ingredient evidence does not automatically establish a result for a finished product. Keep material qualifications with the claim. Do not use customer reviews as clinical proof.

## Capture the real brand

Inspect the official homepage and product page on desktop and mobile. Use the site's rendered styles or source when available instead of estimating everything from a screenshot.

| Capture | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Logo | Actual asset, light/dark variation, proportion, clear space, header placement |
| Typography | Font family and fallback, available weights, title casing, line height, tracking |
| Colors | Text, background, accent, button, border, and selected-state colors |
| Components | Button shape, content width, spacing, input treatment, image crop, footer structure |
| Photography | Product angle, lighting, background, composition, close-up versus lifestyle use |
| Voice | A few representative headlines, level of explanation, words the brand uses or avoids |

Keep a short brand record and a list of reusable assets with their sources. Use the user's own or authorized material. A publicly accessible file is not permission to copy another brand's content. Use official assets for the brand being represented and respect font/asset usage rights. Do not place a competitor's logo or photography on a page as decoration.

If the font cannot be verified or licensed for reuse, identify a reasonable fallback and disclose the substitution. Do not quietly label it the brand font. If you can only see screenshots, distinguish observed visual choices from verified CSS values.

## Research how people decide

Use accessible first-party reviews, retailer reviews for the exact product, public forums, and category discussions. User-supplied review exports are useful when access is limited. Competitor product pages help verify alternatives; their marketing does not establish what buyers believe.

Adapt searches to the category. Useful starting queries include:

- `[product or brand] reviews`
- `[category] hard to use / worth it / disappointed`
- `[category] vs [alternative]`
- `[buying situation] [category] recommendations`
- `site:reddit.com [category] [specific question]`

Inspect positive and negative experiences, recent and older examples where relevant, and at least one source outside the brand's own marketing when accessible. Look for distinct buying questions rather than collecting many repetitions of one claim. Stop when additional sources repeat established themes and the proposed angle has enough support, or when available evidence has been exhausted.

Extract:

- The situation that triggered the search or purchase.
- The result the person wanted in ordinary language.
- What they tried before and what disappointed them.
- What made them hesitate: cost, effort, trust, fit, delivery, or another concrete concern.
- The product detail or experience that mattered to their decision.

Group themes, retain source links, and look for contradictions. If tallying reviews, deduplicate them, state the sample size and selection method, and count each review at most once per theme. Report counts as observations in that sample. A prominent thread, search ranking, or repeated marketing claim does not establish prevalence across the market.

Keep private customer messages and identifiable details out of a public page unless their use is authorized. Quote accurately and sparingly. Do not turn a paraphrase into a quotation or turn a forum comment into a testimonial from a verified buyer.

## Turn research into a page decision

Summarize the strongest relevant themes and choose an angle supported by both a real buyer concern and a verified product response. For each proposed angle, note the evidence, the main limitation, and the proof the page can show. Prefer an argument you can substantiate now.

Use a compact working brief:

```text
Product and primary action:
Visitor's situation:
Incoming ad/search promise (or assumption):
Chosen angle and why it fits:
Verified supporting facts and sources:
Main objection and response:
Offer and material terms:
Brand cues and available assets:
Page format and section plan:
Missing evidence or launch blockers:
```

Research notes belong in the project. The customer page should contain the explanation, attribution, and qualifications a buyer needs, without exposing research labels or internal strategy narration.

## When tools or sources are missing

If you cannot browse, say so before describing research. Work from supplied pages, screenshots, assets, and review exports; identify what you still need to verify. If one source is blocked, use an accessible substitute and name its provenance. Do not bypass logins or invent findings.

Continue a useful draft with supported material. A missing optional testimonial can be omitted. An unverified core claim or purchase destination must remain a named launch gap. Do not call a draft researched or tested when the required work could not be performed.
