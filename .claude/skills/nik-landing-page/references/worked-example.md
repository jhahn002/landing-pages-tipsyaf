# Example: an easier morning coffee routine

This is a fictional exercise. The product, observations, and offer below are invented teaching inputs. Do not reuse them as real claims, research, testimonials, or brand assets.

## Inputs for the exercise

A fictional coffee brewer has one preset brew button, a removable water tank, and a compact footprint. The exercise supplies photos of those features and an official-style specification sheet. Three fictional review summaries mention frustration with complicated settings; two mention refilling a fixed tank. This tiny sample cannot establish how most coffee buyers feel.

The exercise offer is $89 for a brewer, with a 30-day return window. Delivery timing is unspecified. The incoming ad is about reducing the number of steps in a morning routine.

## Working brief

| Field | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Visitor | Someone who wants coffee before work and dislikes adjusting settings |
| Incoming promise | A simpler morning routine |
| Angle | Fewer settings between you and your morning coffee |
| Evidence | One preset button; removable tank; supplied product photos |
| Main objection | Whether simpler controls still meet their brewing needs |
| Response | Show the actual steps and specifications; describe what can and cannot be adjusted |
| Offer and action | $89; 30-day returns; view the verified purchase option |
| Gap | Delivery timing and real customer proof remain unknown |

This angle fits the supplied ad and features. It does not establish better taste, faster brewing, or a conversion uplift.

## Section plan

| Section | Payoff | Proof or asset |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | Understand the product and the simpler-controls angle | Accurate product photo, one-button detail, stated offer |
| How to use | Know the steps before buying | Actual step sequence with close-up photos |
| Two reasons | Fewer settings to adjust; a tank that lifts out for refilling | Supplied specifications and feature imagery |
| Specifications | Decide whether its capacity and controls fit the routine | Exact supplied specification sheet |
| Purchase and returns | Understand the price, contents, action, and return window | Verified offer terms from the exercise |
| FAQ and footer | Resolve remaining supported questions and find help | Only answers supported by the exercise inputs |

The evidence supports two useful reasons. A five-reason headline would require more distinct, relevant support. The unknown delivery estimate stays in the project gaps instead of becoming "fast shipping."

## Example review notes

Suppose the first build uses a large lifestyle image that pushes the product name and button below a short phone screen. Reduce the image height and simplify the hero so the product, argument, and action are visible together.

Suppose the CTA says "Buy for $89" but opens a product listing with several models. Point it to the correct product or change the interaction so the shopper can reliably choose the intended model. Retest the destination before describing the path as finished.
